# Changelog

## 4.0.1 — unreleased

One rule now covers the display of a mass and of a percentage, in every
edition of a card.

- **The print and the workbook disagreed about a mass.** The workbook gave
  every gram cell a number format that painted the stored precision, so
  33.333333 g read `33.333333` in the workbook and `33.3 g` in the PDF. A
  cook scaling from one edition and a cook scaling from the other weighed
  different amounts of salt.
- **They disagreed about the last digit.** The print rounded half to even on
  the binary float; a spreadsheet rounds half away from zero on the decimal
  value. `2.25 g` printed `2.2 g` where the workbook showed `2.3 g`.
- **They disagreed about a percentage.** The workbook holds a ratio and its
  format multiplies by a hundred at display time, so 33.333333 g against a
  500 g basis read `6.6666666%` in the workbook and `6.7%` in the PDF.
- **A mass below a nanogram read as zero.** The test that decides whether a
  mass is a whole number of grams used a tolerance, and the tolerance
  swallowed every mass under 1e-9 g: `5e-10 g` printed `0 g`, which tells
  a cook to add none of the ingredient. The test is exact now.
- **A live formula cell kept the number format it shipped with.** The
  workbook is a working document, so a cook edits an ingredient and the
  total recalculates. A format frozen at write time painted the new total
  with the old rule, and `0.04 g` under a one-decimal format reads `0.0 g`.

Every gram cell now carries one conditional number format and every
percentage cell carries its own, so the section is chosen from the value the
cell computes. The reading editions are bounded by the same depths and round
the way a spreadsheet rounds, so the PDF and the workbook show the same number
for the same mass.

The suite is 661 passed, 0 skipped in a container with native Calc, so the
native-Calf regressions ran rather than skipping.

Known limitation: the shipped `examples/` artifacts are stale and were not
regenerated by this release.

## 4.0.0 — 2026-11-08

Release candidate; publication and live-host acceptance remain separate gates.

- Research-first intake contract: one bounded read of the whole page, the dish
  map before any number, and at most three material questions.
- Bounded comparable-recipe research with recorded sources. At most 3 comparable
  recipes per component, at most 8 comparable technique sources in a run, at
  most 2 volume sources per ingredient, and a whole-run budget of 12 search or
  fetch attempts or 4 minutes. Each source records what was taken from it and
  what was rejected.
- Ingredient-specific volume research with source records: title, URL, access
  date, ratio, units convention, state match, proxy status and confidence.
  Weights stay authoritative; potent ingredients are weighed, not spooned.
- Component cards with independent percentage bases, computed from the stored
  weights within the component and never stored on the card.
- One canonical card record produces the Markdown master, the HTML edition, a
  native PDF and a native XLSX, with a build manifest binding the emitted
  bytes to that record.
- Five editorial themes and five languages, with language-tagged portable
  translations.
- Workflow completeness ledger: declared coverage with required dispositions,
  validated against the card.
- Null-yield and spreadsheet arithmetic guards with real recalculation checks.
- Build-time artifact manifest and retry-safe local filing integrity checks.
- Isolated dependency install, customized-file backup, public source selection,
  deterministic release archive and source SHA-256 manifest.
- One-command install: `python3 scripts/install.py`, with the activation line
  printed for every supported host.
- Per-host run records in `references/host-results.md`, including the checks
  that failed and the checks that were not run. No host is certified by this
  release.
- Contributor guidance, MIT software license, content permission policy and CI.
