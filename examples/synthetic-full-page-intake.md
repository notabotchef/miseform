# Synthetic full-page intake fixture

This is an original fictional demonstration, not a real recipe, a real photo, or
a cook test. It exists so the research-first contract can be exercised against
a realistic whole page: several blocks, one dish, one shared component, one
purchased main, one quantity conflict, and one value nobody can measure.

Nothing here is a real kitchen's recipe. The dish, the products, and the
quantities are invented for the demonstration.

## The page the cook photographs

One sheet of paper, photographed in one frame. Three blocks are written on it,
side by side. The cook says: "this is the whole page for the dinner — the
sauce, the crumb, and the chicken we buy in."

### Block 1 — "Sauce for the chicken"

    Creamy garlic emulsion
    300 ml chicken stock
    180 ml heavy cream
    25 g garlic, sliced thin
    150 g unsalted butter, cubed
    5 g xanthan gum
    Salt to taste

    Method: simmer stock, cream and garlic 12 min. Blend. Whisk in butter
    cube by cube off the heat. Add xanthan, blend 30 s. Salt to taste.

### Block 2 — "Crumb"

    120 g panko
    40 g toasted sesame
    8 g fine salt
    15 g neutral oil

    Method: toast panko and sesame in the oil until dry and golden. Salt.
    Cool on a tray before the tins.

### Block 3 — "Chicken"

    2 x purchased roast chicken breast, 180 g each, chilled
    (the chef's note: "we buy these already cooked, they just need slicing
    and warming through, 12 min at 60 C to reach 63 C core")

At the bottom of the sheet, in the margin: "use the crumb also on the
roast carrots — same batch."

## What the cook says with it

"Standardize the whole page for the dinner service. Use my quantities, don't
change them. I have no idea what the sauce should yield and I have not
weighed the tins, so leave the yield off if you cannot measure it. Make me the
card."

## What this fixture is meant to prove

1. The page is one dish with three components, and the crumb is shared with
   another dish — the map says so before any arithmetic.
2. The chicken is purchased. No cooking process is invented for it, and the
   chef's stated 12 minutes at 60 C to 63 C core is preserved as stated.
3. "Salt to taste" is routine technique, so it is researched and proposed
   rather than asked.
4. "I have not weighed the tins" is an unavailable yield: it stays unknown and
   is not asked again.
5. At most three material questions come back, and they are the ones that
   change reproducibility or safety.
