SESAME SALT GARNISH

A loose, lightly crushed sesame finish for grilled vegetables. White sesame brings the body; whole black seeds add contrast. Stop crushing while the mixture still falls freely from a spoon.

Method

CRUSH

  1. 1

    Weigh white sesame (I1) into the 300 mL mortar. Use 10 press-and-turn strokes, approximately 30 seconds from the first stroke.

  2. 2

    Check the texture: roughly half the seeds should split. The mixture must remain loose, with no oily paste.

Roughly half the seeds should split. The mixture must stay loose, with no oily paste.

MIX

  1. 3

    Add black sesame (I2) and salt (I3). Stir with a spoon for 20 turns, approximately 20 seconds from the first turn. Total mortar load: 130 g.Settings — speed rpm spoon stirring, 20 turns

  2. 4

    Check that black seeds are distributed through the mixture and no salt pockets remain. Log any failed endpoint for trial review.

WEIGH & PORTION

  1. 5

    Scrape into a tared, dry container. Record actual usable mass and transfer loss. Reference trial: 128 g usable from 130 g input.

  2. 6

    For the simulated plating trial, weigh 2.0 g per plate and sprinkle evenly across the vegetable surface immediately before dispatch. No bowl garnish.

Volume research and sources

Volumes are researched approximations, not measurements; gram weights stay authoritative. Source 1: King Arthur Baking, Ingredient Weight Chart, https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/learn/ingredient-weight-chart, accessed 2026-10-26, listing sesame seeds at 1/2 US cup = 71 g, that is 8.875 g per US tablespoon, with US measures (1 US cup = 236.6 mL, 1 US tbsp = 14.79 mL, 1 US tsp = 4.93 mL). Source 2: USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy 173468, Salt, table, https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173468/nutrients, accessed 2026-10-26, listing 1 tsp = 6 g, 1 tbsp = 18 g and 1 cup = 292 g in US measures. Units convention: US tablespoon, teaspoon and cup throughout. An Australian 20 mL tablespoon is not mixed with these figures, and no metric measure is used on this card. Proxy status: rows I1 and I2 use the generic whole-sesame-seed ratio because the purchased brand and roast state were not provided, so both are labeled proxies rather than exact matches; row I3 uses the generic table-salt ratio because the salt type was not provided. Fine and kosher salts are not interchangeable - table salt at 18 g per US tbsp, Morton's kosher at 16 g and Diamond Crystal at 8 g, a spread of about 2.25 times for the same 10 g - so the salt volume is a cross-check only and the 10 g weight remains the production control.

Volume arithmetic and rounding

I1: 100 g divided by 8.875 g per US tbsp = 11.27 tbsp, shown as approximately 11.3 tbsp, rounded to the nearest 0.1 tbsp. I2: 20 g divided by 8.875 g per US tbsp = 2.25 tbsp, shown as approximately 2.25 tbsp, rounded to the nearest 0.05 tbsp. I3: 10 g divided by 6 g per US tsp = 1.67 tsp, shown as approximately 1.7 tsp, rounded to the nearest 0.1 tsp. Cup equivalents at 142 g per US cup are approximately 0.70 cup for I1 and approximately 0.14 cup for I2. Every volume on this card is a derived approximation: where an exact measure is needed, weigh the ingredient and measure the spoon.
